# Toward Reliable Evaluation of LLM-Based Financial Multi-Agent Systems: Taxonomy, Coordination Primacy, and Cost Awareness

- **ID:** arXiv:2603.27539 (v1, 29 Mar 2026, cs.MA) — verified from https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27539v1 (full text)
- **Authors:** Phat Nguyen (Georgia Institute of Technology), Thang Pham (Adobe Inc.)
- **Status:** EXCLUDE — code E1 (markets/trading). All 12 surveyed MAS are trading/portfolio-allocation systems; zero CFO-office task coverage. Recommend retaining as contrast-class / evaluation-methodology citation (RQ3), consistent with existing survey-contrast items in screening.csv.

## Summary
Analytical survey (no new empirical results) of LLM-based multi-agent systems for financial trading. Contributions per abstract: (1) "a four-dimensional taxonomy, covering architecture pattern, coordination mechanism, memory architecture, and tool integration; applied to 12 multi-agent systems and two single-agent baselines"; (2) the Coordination Primacy Hypothesis (CPH) — "inter-agent coordination protocol design is a primary driver of trading decision quality, often exerting greater influence than model scaling" — framed as falsifiable, not validated; (3) "five pervasive evaluation failures (look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, backtesting overfitting, transaction cost neglect, and regime-shift blindness)" which "can reverse the sign of reported returns"; plus the Coordination Breakeven Spread (CBS) metric and minimum evaluation standards.

## Tasks
No benchmark tasks; taxonomy applied to 12 trading MAS (FinCon, TradingAgents, HedgeAgents, ContestTrade, FinVision, TradingGPT, QuantAgents, AlphaAgents, ElliottAgents, FinRobot, Agentic RAG, Chinese Public REITs system) + 2 single-agent baselines (FinMem, FinAgent) (Sec 3.1). Inclusion criterion for surveyed systems: "employ distinct LLM roles for active trading or portfolio allocation" — i.e., markets scope by construction.
