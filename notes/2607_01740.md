## Summary
A meta-benchmarking framework for model selection/governance in financial services. "It introduces no new benchmarks. Instead, it aggregates 452 existing publicly reported benchmarks into 41 work activities drawn from the O*NET Generalized Work Activities taxonomy ... and further aggregates those into 38 banking business domains drawn from the BIAN Service Landscape 14.0.0" (Sec 1). Scores are sourced from the LLM Stats API (llm-stats.com), June 2026 snapshot. Per-domain model scores are computed via pairwise Elo with a dynamic K-factor weighting (discrimination x coverage x recency). Validation: K-factor sensitivity, factor ablation, rank divergence vs global composites, and evidence density per domain. Applications: model selection, procurement, concentration risk, governance/frontier tracking.

## Exactly what they map
- Pyramid (Fig. 1 caption): "288+ models are scored on 452 public benchmarks, which are mapped to 41 O*NET Generalized Work Activities, aggregated into 38 BIAN business domains, and grouped under five BIAN Business Areas."
- Mapping unit: PUBLIC BENCHMARK IDENTIFIERS WITH REPORTED SCORES -> generic work activities -> banking business domains. Many-to-many; "The total number of benchmark–work-activity assignments is 1,750" (Sec 3.4).
- Mapping basis: "Task assignments are made on the basis of what the benchmark primarily measures, not the domain of its questions" (Sec 4.1).
- Coverage facts: "only 24 of the 41 GWAs are exercised by any public benchmark" (Sec 6.3); "288 models from 25 organisations spanning the period January 2022 to June 2026" (Sec 3.2).
- Benchmarks named include MMLU/MMLU-Pro, SWE-bench Verified, tau-bench, Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard, OSWorld, FinQA, FinanceBench, BANKING77 (Secs 2.4-2.5, 4.1) — i.e., general + a thin finance-NLP layer; agentic benchmarks appear as generic capability evidence (Tool Use, Coding, Vision & GUI), not as finance-workflow evidence.

## Contribution claims (quoted)
- Sec 1: "The contribution is fourfold. First, we articulate a principled taxonomy mapping benchmarks to standardised work activities and banking business domains, grounded in established external standards (O*NET and BIAN). Second, we describe a weighting and normalisation methodology that handles the heterogeneous and rapidly evolving landscape of public benchmarks without requiring manual score curation. Third, we describe practical applications of the resulting capability profiles in preliminary model comparison, risk-informed screening, and governance research. Fourth, we provide sufficient methodological detail to make the taxonomy and aggregation approach reproducible..."
- Sec 10: "Its central contribution is a dynamic weighting scheme (discrimination × coverage × recency) that automatically promotes benchmarks actively contesting frontier performance and suppresses saturated legacy tests, without manual score curation or benchmark lifecycle management."
- Sec 10 caveat: "The benchmark-to-activity and activity-to-domain mappings are a candidate mapping but not a validated ground truth."

## Differentiation (our survey vs 2607.01740)
1. **Different mapping axis and unit of analysis.** They map benchmark SCORE records (452 public benchmarks, mostly general-purpose: MMLU, SWE-bench, OSWorld...) through occupation-generic O*NET activities into BIAN BANKING domains, to produce per-domain MODEL Elo rankings for model selection/procurement. Our coverage matrix maps the CONTENT of LLM/agent benchmarks and systems (task instances, eval methods, data realism, agent scope) onto a purpose-built CFO-office task taxonomy to show which finance-function tasks are and are not measured — a benchmark-gap analysis, not a model leaderboard.
2. **No CFO-office taxonomy.** Their finest-grained treatment of the finance function is a single "Finance" domain (one of 38, Business Support area) with no sub-structure; our taxonomy decomposes the CFO office into ~40 leaf tasks (JEs, reconciliation, consolidation, close orchestration, SOX, XBRL, budget/forecast/variance, treasury, tax, IR, P2P/O2C, audit, ERP automation). They never use the words CFO, accounting, close, invoice, or XBRL.
3. **Different corpus.** Theirs requires publicly reported scores on llm-stats.com and explicitly "introduces no new benchmarks" nor screens papers; ours is a PRISMA full-text screen of finance-function agent benchmarks/systems, including works with no leaderboard presence (e.g., invoice-extraction systems, month-end-close orchestration, XBRL tagging benchmarks).
4. **Complementary evidence for our thesis.** Their own validation concedes sparse public evidence exactly where we look: specialist Finance/Regulations-dependent domains "have notably lower model evidence density, reflecting a genuine gap in the public evaluation landscape for regulated domain tasks" (Sec 7.4) — corroborating, at coarse grain, the CFO-office coverage gap our matrix quantifies at leaf-task grain.
5. **Methodological contrast to cite.** Their external-standards argument (O*NET/BIAN "reproducible and resistant to the selection bias of custom taxonomies", Sec 6.1) is a design position our paper should engage: we should justify the bespoke CFO taxonomy by mapping it to recognized finance-function frameworks (e.g., process classification) and note their mappings are self-described "candidate ... not a validated ground truth" (Sec 10).
