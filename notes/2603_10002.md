# SpreadsheetArena: Decomposing Preference in LLM Generation of Spreadsheet Workbooks

- **ID:** arXiv:2603.10002 (v1, 16 Feb 2026) — verified from https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10002 + https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10002v1 (full text)
- **Authors:** Srivatsa Kundurthy, Clara Na, Michael Handley, Zach Kirshner, Chen Bo Calvin Zhang, Manasi Sharma, Emma Strubell, John Ling
- **Status:** INCLUDE — category: cross-domain (general spreadsheet-generation arena with a distinct, expert-evaluated finance-function subset; analysis restricted to that subset)

## Summary
Live arena platform for blind pairwise evaluation of end-to-end LLM spreadsheet-workbook generation: users submit natural-language prompts, vote between anonymous generated workbooks; Bradley-Terry/Elo rankings over 16 models from 4,357 votes. Complementary analyses: 29 programmatic artifact features regressed on win probability, LLM-clustered failure taxonomy, and a finance-domain expert rubric study.

## Finance-function subset (basis for cross-domain include)
- Seed-prompt taxonomy (436 expert-authored prompts) includes **"Corporate Finance & Financial Planning and Analysis (FP&A)"** and **"Professional Finance"** as 2 of 6 categories (Sec 4.3).
- Sec 5.4 Finance Domain Expert Evaluation Study: 52 finance-domain battles (104 spreadsheets); "Prompts span canonical financial workflows, including DCFs, LBOs, and distribution waterfalls." Five evaluators with 2+ years Excel-based financial modeling experience (investment banking / private equity), blinded, 6-dimension 5-point rubric incl. "Financial Modeling Conventions" and "Structure & Organization ... auditability".

## Leaf cells
- **2.5 ● (F)** — FP&A modeling/spreadsheets: dedicated Corporate Finance & FP&A prompt category and expert study of financial-model workbook generation against professional modeling conventions.
- **ADJ ◐ (P)** — Professional Finance prompts (DCF/LBO/distribution-waterfall, IB/PE evaluators) sit in the banker/analyst band rather than the CFO office proper.

## Eval method
Blind pairwise human preference votes → Bradley-Terry ability parameters rescaled to Elo (GPT-4o anchored at 1000); observable-feature-adjusted scores; data-driven failure taxonomy from clustered LLM loss rationales; expert rubric study (5-point Likert x 6 dimensions; 134 evaluations; Krippendorff's alpha 0.27–0.51 on triply-rated subset).

## Data realism
Real user-submitted prompts on a live public arena plus 436 expert-authored seed prompts; artifacts are LLM-generated workbooks (real intents, synthetic outputs). No real corporate data.

## Agent scope
Single-shot LLM generation of complete .xlsx workbooks from a prompt (16 models). Not tool-using or iterative agents; no enterprise-system integration.

## Key numbers (quoted)
- Abstract: "expert evaluations of spreadsheets for finance prompts suggests that even highly ranked arena models do not reliably produce spreadsheets aligned with domain-specific best practices."
- Sec 5.4: "The mean overall rating was 2.87 (SD=0.87), slightly below the midpoint (3 = acceptable). Only 23.1% of evaluations scored >=4."
- Sec 5.4: weakest dimension Color Coding & Formatting "M=1.95 ... 77.6% scoring <=2"; "No model consistently followed established professional formatting standards (e.g., blue hard-codes/assumptions, black formulas/calculations, green cross-sheet links)."
- Sec 5.4: "Among decisive comparisons, agreement [expert vs arena] was 56.4%, only modestly above chance."
- Conclusion: "crowd-sourced preferences agree with expert judgments in finance only modestly."

## Survey use
Key evidence for the 2.5 modeling cell and for RQ (eval methodology): crowd preference vs domain-expert rubric divergence in finance; documents that frontier models fail professional financial-modeling conventions. Restrict CFO-office claims to the finance prompt categories / Sec 5.4 study.
